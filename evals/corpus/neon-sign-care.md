# Neon Sign Care

The neon signs on the street front and in the lounge are part of the brand. They are glass tubes filled with low-pressure gas and driven by high voltage.

## How the colors work

Pure neon gas glows red-orange. Argon with a drop of mercury glows blue. Other colors come from phosphor coatings on the inside of the glass that are excited by the ultraviolet light from the argon-mercury discharge.

## Transformers and voltage

Neon tubes run on several thousand volts supplied by a neon sign transformer or an electronic power supply. Modern installations require a transformer with ground-fault protection. Only a licensed sign technician may open a transformer housing or touch the electrode connections.

## Common faults

Flickering usually means a failing transformer or a loose electrode connection. A tube that glows dimly in one section may have lost gas through a hairline crack. Pink or purple tint in a red tube means the gas is contaminated. Buzzing from the transformer is normal at a low level, but a loud buzz means the transformer is overloaded.

## Cleaning

Switch the sign off and let the tubes cool for at least thirty minutes. Dust the glass with a soft dry microfiber cloth. Do not use water or spray cleaners near the electrodes.
