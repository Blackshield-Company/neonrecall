# Drum Machine Programming

Drum machines provide the pulse of every track. These are the house conventions for programming patterns.

## Step sequencing

Most patterns are programmed on a 16-step grid where each step is a sixteenth note. Kick on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 gives four-on-the-floor; snare on steps 5 and 13 gives the backbeat.

## Swing

Swing delays every second sixteenth note to create a shuffling groove. A swing setting of 50 percent is straight time; 54 to 58 percent gives a subtle human feel without sounding like a shuffle. Apply swing to hi-hats and percussion first, and leave the kick straight.

## Accents and velocity

Accent raises the level of selected steps. Program accents on off-beat hi-hats to make a pattern breathe. On machines with velocity, vary hi-hat velocity between about 70 and 110 instead of keeping every hit at the maximum.

## Gated reverb snare

The signature eighties snare is a big reverb cut off abruptly by a noise gate. Send the snare to a bright plate or room reverb, put a gate on the reverb return keyed from the dry snare, and set the gate hold to roughly 200 to 300 milliseconds.
