# CRT Monitor Maintenance

Cathode-ray tube displays power the arcade floor and the studio's preview monitors. They are durable but need periodic care.

## Safety first

A CRT can hold a dangerous charge on the anode for days after it is unplugged. Before opening a chassis, discharge the anode cap to the chassis ground using a grounded flathead screwdriver and a clip lead. Never work on a monitor alone, and keep one hand in your pocket when probing a live flyback circuit.

## Degaussing

Magnetized shadow masks cause patches of discoloration, usually near the corners. Most sets degauss automatically at power-on through a thermistor-controlled coil. For stubborn purity blotches use a handheld degaussing wand: switch it on a metre away, move it in slow circles toward the screen, then back away slowly before switching it off. Switching the wand off near the tube leaves the mask more magnetized than before.

## Convergence and geometry

Convergence is the alignment of the red, green and blue beams. Misconvergence shows up as colored fringes around white text. Static convergence is adjusted with the ring magnets on the neck of the tube; dynamic convergence is adjusted on the convergence board. Use a crosshatch test pattern and fix the center of the screen before the edges.

## Capacitor replacement

Monitors older than about fifteen years often suffer dried-out electrolytic capacitors, which cause wavy pictures, poor focus or a collapsed vertical picture that shows only a single horizontal line. A full cap kit for the chassis is cheap; replace capacitors one at a time and observe polarity.
