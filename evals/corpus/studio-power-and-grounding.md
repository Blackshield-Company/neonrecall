# Studio Power and Grounding

Clean power keeps the noise floor low and the gear alive.

## Ground loops

A ground loop happens when two devices are connected to ground through different paths, so a small current flows through the audio shield. The result is a steady hum at the mains frequency, 50 Hz in Europe and 60 Hz in North America, plus harmonics. Plug all audio gear into outlets on the same circuit, and use balanced cables or an isolation transformer to break the loop. Never remove the earth pin from a power cable to cure hum.

## Power conditioning

Every rack has a surge protector and a power conditioner. During thunderstorms the rule is to power down and unplug the modular rack entirely.

## Power-up order

Switch on sources first and amplifiers and studio monitors last. Power down in the reverse order: monitors first. This prevents loud thumps that can damage speaker cones.

## Load limits

A single 15 amp circuit should not carry more than about 80 percent of its rating continuously. The vintage tube amps draw heavily at switch-on, so stagger their startup by a few seconds.
