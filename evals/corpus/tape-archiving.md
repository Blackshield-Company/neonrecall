# Tape Archiving

The vault holds master recordings on reel-to-reel, cassette and VHS. Magnetic tape degrades if stored badly.

## Storage conditions

Store tape at 15 to 20 degrees Celsius and 30 to 40 percent relative humidity. Keep temperature and humidity stable; rapid swings are worse than slightly high values. Store reels and cassettes vertically, like books, never stacked flat.

## Sticky-shed syndrome

Some tapes from the 1970s and 1980s absorb moisture into the binder, which then turns gummy and sheds oxide onto the heads. The symptom is a squealing sound during playback and brown residue on the tape path. The temporary fix is baking: heat the tape at about 54 degrees Celsius for several hours in a dedicated, accurately controlled oven, then digitize it immediately.

## Digitization workflow

Clean and demagnetize the heads before every transfer session. Capture audio at 96 kHz and 24-bit as uncompressed WAV. Record a reference tone at the start of each reel and note the tape speed, equalization standard and noise reduction used on the label.

## Labeling

Each archived item gets a catalog number, the date of transfer and a checksum of the digital file. Checksums are verified once a year to catch silent corruption on the archive drives.
